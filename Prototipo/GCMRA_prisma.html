Nonlinear Attitude Dynamics and Control of a CMG-Actuated Spacecraft

This project investigates the nonlinear attitude dynamics and closed-loop control of a spacecraft actuated by a single Control Moment Gyroscope (CMG). The system is modeled as a multibody rigid-body structure composed of three interconnected elements: the spacecraft bus, the CMG gimbal frame, and the high-speed rotor. The resulting configuration leads to a five-degree-of-freedom mechanical system, where three generalized coordinates describe the spacecraft attitude motion, while the remaining coordinates correspond to the internal CMG dynamics. Since the CMG acts as the sole actuator, the spacecraft constitutes an underactuated dynamical system, making the control problem significantly more challenging than conventional reaction-wheel-based architectures. The equations of motion were derived using both the Euler–Lagrange formalism and a geometric algebra representation of rigid-body kinematics, providing a compact and coordinate-consistent description of the multibody dynamics. This formulation allows the spacecraft–CMG system to be expressed in the classical robotic manipulator form

\(M \ddot{\boldsymbol{q}} + C\dot{\boldsymbol{q}} = B \boldsymbol{\tau} \),

which enables the systematic application of >nonlinear control methodologies for underactuated mechanical systems. The simulation implements a closed-loop attitude control strategy designed to stabilize the spacecraft around a desired reference orientation. Starting from an arbitrary initial condition, the controller drives the spacecraft attitude toward the commanded reference while maintaining the internal CMG dynamics bounded. The animation illustrates the resulting behavior of the system: the spacecraft attitude converges smoothly to the target orientation, while the internal configuration variables remain dynamically stable throughout the maneuver. This demonstrates the feasibility of single-CMG attitude control for spacecraft stabilization, as well as the effectiveness of the nonlinear control approach applied to the coupled spacecraft–CMG dynamics.
